Huailu Li

Associate Professor of Economics
Fudan University

huailu@bu.edu

I am an applied microeconomist whose research examines individual behavior and social interactions in markets and institutional settings that are often difficult to observe. My work spans the economics of crime, household finance, and labor economics, with a particular interest in how incentives, social norms, and institutions shape behavior.

Much of my research is built around original data and field partnerships. I have studied illegal credit and illicit markets, discrimination, and behavior in correctional institutions, using a combination of field experiments and administrative records. More recently, my research has expanded to social mobility and social networks, where I am developing a long-run panel study to understand how social and economic circumstances and networks affect social mobility over time.
